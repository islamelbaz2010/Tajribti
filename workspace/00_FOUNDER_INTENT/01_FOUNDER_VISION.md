## THE GENESIS

A founder watched a 3-minute video. In Madrid, people stood in a queue at a temporary Mentos pop-up. They had downloaded an app called Samplia. The app notified them that free Mentos samples were available at Gran Via. They went, they picked up the product, they tried it, and they rated it inside the app. Mentos received consumer feedback. The consumers received a free product. Samplia — the platform operator — charged Mentos for the service. That model had been running in Spain for ten years.

The insight was immediate: Egyptian FMCG brands run product sampling campaigns and receive nothing back. No consumer profile. No purchase intent. No first-impression opinion. Just a reach number nobody can act on.

**Tajribti is that model, built for Egypt.**

## THE IDEA IN 60 SECONDS

Egyptian FMCG brands invest in product sampling and get nothing useful back. Tajribti solves this.

A brand pays to place its product at a retail location in Cairo. Consumers download the app, show up, receive a free product, try it, and answer five questions on their phone. Within 24 hours, the brand has a consumer intelligence report: who tried the product, what they thought, how likely they are to buy it — broken down by demographic profile.

The brand pays per campaign. The consumer gets a free product. Tajribti keeps the data infrastructure and the growing consumer panel. The panel becomes more valuable with every campaign. That is the business.

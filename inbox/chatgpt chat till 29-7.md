# ROLE

You are performing an independent peer review of another AI's consulting report.

You already created your own complete analysis independently.

You have now received another AI's report.

Your task is NOT to rewrite it.

Your task is to perform a professional consulting peer review exactly as an Investment Committee would.

Assume both reports were prepared independently.

Your goal is to produce a stronger final version by combining the best parts of both reports.

---

# REVIEW RULES

Never assume your own report is correct.

Never assume the other report is correct.

Challenge both.

Every conclusion must be justified.

When two reports disagree:

Determine which conclusion is stronger.

Explain WHY.

If neither is sufficiently supported,

create a better conclusion.

---

# STEP 1

Read both reports completely.

Understand:

- Facts
- Assumptions
- Estimates
- Opinions

Identify contradictions.

---

# STEP 2

Create a comparison matrix.

For every section compare:

Coverage

Accuracy

Depth

Logic

Evidence

Business realism

Financial realism

Market realism

Implementation feasibility

Risk awareness

Innovation

Actionability

Score each section from both reports.

---

# STEP 3

Identify:

Information missing in Report A

Information missing in Report B

Weak arguments

Unsupported assumptions

Incorrect conclusions

Hallucinations

Missing risks

Missing competitors

Missing financial assumptions

Missing implementation details

Missing legal considerations

Missing operational considerations

Missing market insights

---

# STEP 4

Merge both reports.

Keep ONLY the strongest version of every section.

Rewrite where necessary.

Remove duplication.

Resolve contradictions.

Improve explanations.

Fill every gap.

Expand weak sections.

Preserve all valuable ideas from both reports.

---

# STEP 5

Create a FINAL MASTER REPORT.

This should NOT look like two merged reports.

It must read as one professionally written consulting document.

The reader should never know two reports existed.

---

# STEP 6

Perform one final self-review.

Ask:

"If I were an Investment Committee reviewing a multi-million-dollar investment, what would still be missing?"

List every remaining weakness.

Improve the report again.

Repeat until no major weaknesses remain.

---

# OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS

Deliver:

1. Executive Comparison Matrix

2. Strengths of Report A

3. Strengths of Report B

4. Weaknesses of Report A

5. Weaknesses of Report B

6. Newly Added Insights

7. Final Master Report

8. Investment Committee Review

9. Confidence Score

10. Remaining Unknowns

The final deliverable must be objectively stronger than either original report.

Do not preserve wording from either report unless it is already the clearest possible expression.

Write as if this report will determine whether millions of dollars will be invested.

IMPLEMENTATION BACKLOG — TRACK 0 SALES EXECUTION PACK

Scope: 7 tasks. Maximum 10 authorized. All 7 required. None are optional.
Sorted by: Proximity to first LOI signature.

---
TASK 1 — LinkedIn Outreach Message

Business Impact: Without this, the sprint does not start. First contact precedes everything.
Output: One 3-sentence LinkedIn message. Cold open. Specific to FMCG brand profile.
Why existing assets cannot solve this: GO_TO_MARKET.md contains a 300-word sales strategy and positioning rationale. A brand's marketing director cannot receive a strategy document. The gap is not knowledge — it is synthesis into a sendable message that passes the "would a real person send this?" test.

---
TASK 2 — Email Outreach Template

Business Impact: Parallel channel to Task 1. Different format, different decision-maker path, same Day 1 urgency.
Output: Subject line + 5-sentence body + single CTA. Two variants: warm intro and cold outreach.
Why existing assets cannot solve this: Same source material as Task 1, different format requirements. Email requires a subject line with ≤50 characters, a formal opening, and explicit next-step language. No version of this exists in the repository.

---
TASK 3 — Brand Pitch One-Pager

Business Impact: Sent with or immediately after first contact. Converts "curious reply" into "booked discovery call." Without it, interested brands have nothing to read between the outreach and the call.
Output: One page. Covers: what the platform does, what brands receive, why it is different from Marketeers Research, one illustrative outcome, and a call-to-action.
Why existing assets cannot solve this: GO_TO_MARKET.md contains every factual input this document needs — positioning, differentiation, competitive narrative, target ROI story. It cannot be handed to an external brand contact because it is written in strategy-document voice, not brand-client voice, and it is 2,000 words across multiple sections. The gap is reformatting and voice.

---
TASK 4 — Discovery Call Guide

Business Impact: The call is where the LOI is won or lost. A structured guide converts a 30-minute conversation into a qualified commitment. Without structure, calls produce "interesting, let us think about it" — not LOIs.
Output: Call agenda (4 stages), opening questions, pricing and WTP discovery questions, objection responses for the top 3 objections ("we already work with Marketeers Research," "our sampling budget is allocated," "too early to commit"), and a defined close ("can we send you a draft LOI to review?").
Why existing assets cannot solve this: The ChatGPT prompt library (11_Prompts/CHATGPT_PROMPTS.md) is an 18-phase investment analysis framework — designed for researching a business, not for selling to one. GO_TO_MARKET.md has the competitive differentiation narrative but no call structure, no questions, and no objection handling. Nothing in the repository addresses a live sales conversation.

---
TASK 5 — LOI Template

Business Impact: The LOI template IS the commercial close instrument. Even if the founder executes Tasks 1–4 perfectly, no LOI can be signed without a document to sign. This is the legal instrument that converts a verbal "yes" into a measurable sprint outcome.
Output: 1–2 page letter of intent. Covers: parties, pilot campaign scope, timeline (60-day), commercial terms (fee range, payment), data usage rights, exclusivity (or non-exclusivity), and mutual termination clause.
Why existing assets cannot solve this: No LOI template or contract template exists anywhere in the repository. The PRD describes campaign features; the FDD describes commercial terms philosophy; the IC Memo quotes illustrative price ranges ($4K–$20K). None of these are signable instruments. Building requires synthesis of the commercial terms from the FDD and IC Memo into legal-format language.

---
TASK 6 — Egyptian LLC Incorporation Checklist

Business Impact: B-02 (LLC incorporation) is a blocking condition for Sprint 0. Without a registered entity, no vendor contracts (SMS, WhatsApp BSP, AWS, payment providers) can be signed. This checklist gives the founder the exact sequence of steps to close B-02 in parallel with outreach, without engaging a lawyer for general guidance at hourly rates.
Output: Ordered checklist of ~10–15 steps. Covers: entity type (LLC vs. SAE), registration authority (GAFI one-stop-shop), capital requirements, timeline, required documents, post-registration steps (commercial register, tax card, bank account).
Why existing assets cannot solve this: B-02 is listed as open and blocking in 15_Decisions/OPEN_DECISIONS_TRACKER.md and AI_BOOTSTRAP/02_PROJECT_STATE.md. Neither file tells the founder what to actually do. The 02_Project_Management/MASTER_DELIVERY_PLAN.md places LLC formation in Sprint 0 but defines no steps for achieving it. The gap is the procedural path, not the decision.

---
TASK 7 — PDPL Lawyer Engagement Brief

Business Impact: B-03 (PDPL written legal sign-off) cannot be obtained without engaging an Egyptian data-privacy lawyer. A lawyer cannot be engaged without a written scope brief. Without PDPL sign-off, no data-collecting feature can ship — which means no campaign reports, which means no brand renewals.
Output: One-page brief covering: platform description (physical trial → QR scan → survey → data report), consumer data categories collected, consent mechanism design, data residency question (AWS Bahrain), and the 4 specific questions requiring a written opinion.
Why existing assets cannot solve this: PDPL content exists in the PRD (TJ-008 Consent & Privacy Center), the Technical Architecture, and the FDD. These are 30,000+ word technical documents written for engineers, not for a lawyer intake meeting. A lawyer needs a focused scope question, not a system architecture. The gap is the brief format and the four specific legal questions — not the underlying content.

# TAJRIBTI
## Egypt's Consumer Intelligence Platform

---

### THE PROBLEM

You invest in product sampling. You get reach numbers back.

You do not know who tried your product. You do not know what they thought. You cannot prove to your management that the spend produced anything useful.

Traditional market research gives you an answer — but it takes 3 to 6 weeks, costs significant budget, and surveys people from memory, not from experience.

By the time the data arrives, your launch decision has already been made without it.

---

### THE SOLUTION

Tajribti converts product trials into structured consumer intelligence within 24 hours.

Here is how it works:

Your product is placed at a physical retail location in Cairo. A consumer picks it up, tries it, and completes a short survey on their phone. Their profile — demographic, purchase intent, product feedback — is captured at the moment of first experience, before any purchase decision.

Within 24 hours of the campaign, you receive a data report.

---

### WHAT YOU RECEIVE

**A consumer intelligence report containing:**

- **Who tried your product** — age, gender, area, income segment
- **What they thought** — ratings on taste, packaging, value, and attributes specific to your product
- **Purchase intent** — how likely they are to buy after the trial
- **Open feedback** — in their own words, in Arabic, unfiltered
- **Segment breakdowns** — which consumer profiles responded best

Every data point is linked to an actual trial of your actual product. Not a recall survey. Not a focus group. First-impression, in-moment consumer data.

---

### BUSINESS VALUE

**For your marketing team:** Validate consumer response before committing to national distribution. Know which segments to target and which attributes to emphasize.

**For your management:** A defensible ROI narrative. $X in campaign spend produced Y consumer data points from Z demographics. That is a number you can take upstairs.

**For your brand:** Speed. A launch decision informed by real consumer data — not guesswork — made in days, not weeks.

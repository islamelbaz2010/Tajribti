## Summary

The IC Memo recommends a **Conditional GO** — specifically to fund a bounded, cheap discovery phase (not the full build) with explicit go/no-go criteria before any further capital is committed. The reframing of the business as a Consumer Intelligence Platform (where sampling is merely the acquisition mechanic) is the single most important strategic insight.

---

## Recommendation

> **Conditional GO**

Fund a bounded, cheap discovery phase — not the full build — with explicit go/no-go criteria before any further capital is committed.

The Committee does not recommend investing in the concept exactly as observed in the reference video. Instead, it recommends investing in the validation and development of a broader Consumer Intelligence Platform.

---

## Investment Thesis (IC Version)

> The investment opportunity is the infrastructure built around it.

The long-term value lies in owning a platform capable of generating first-party consumer intelligence through real-world consumer interactions. Rather than competing as an experiential marketing agency, the venture should become a technology-enabled intelligence company serving brands.

## Core Assumptions the Model Depends On

1. Consumers will complete a short post-trial survey in exchange for a free product
2. Brands will pay a premium for attributable, tagged sampling data over traditional market research
3. The cost of distributing a sample is lower than the value of the data it generates
4. A large enough panel of verified, repeat users can be built for segment-level statistical usefulness

---

## Egypt Market Assessment

Egypt fits the underlying category (young, mall-centric, social-media-active, urban population with rising FMCG innovation activity) but **no bottom-up market sizing has yet been built** for this specific product.

**Priority segments:** FMCG, food, cosmetics, personal care, pharma OTC, beverage brands targeting consumers aged 18–40 in Cairo, Giza, Alexandria.

## Go-to-Market Sequence

1. **Stage 1 — Brand supply:** Target ~20 named companies (Coca-Cola Egypt, PepsiCo, Nestlé, Edita, Juhayna, Unilever, P&G, Henkel, EVA Pharma, Dabur, L'Oréal, Beiersdorf). Sell measurable results, not software.
2. **Stage 2 — Consumer acquisition:** Only after first brand pilots are secured.
3. **Stage 3 — Data flywheel:** Panel grows → data quality improves → brand willingness-to-pay increases → reinvest in panel.

## Legal & Regulatory

- **Recommended structure:** Egyptian LLC → JSC as company scales
- **Key statutes:** Companies Law; Consumer Protection Law; PDPL (Law No. 151 of 2020); e-commerce regulations
- **Biggest risk:** Consumer profiling, behavioral analytics, location tracking under PDPL
- **Still open:** Cross-border data-transfer rules, consent mechanics, data-retention periods, incident-response obligations
